### TRAP Candidates

1. **LLM-judge sole reliance as verifier** — Advani (2606.09863) demonstrates 45–75% false-success miss rates with LLM judges in production multi-step agent domains; Zhang et al. (2606.10315) independently confirm zero detection of defects in one-third of blind trials. Using the LLM verifier as the final authority on step completion without a structural fallback (DOM-state, deterministic assertion) is flagged as a potential false-positive propagation trap. Phase 2 should assess whether vQA's tiered verifier (verifier.py) is sufficient or whether the LLM layer is doing structural verification work that should be deterministic.

2. **Fixed recursion_limit as loop prevention** — Hou et al. (2607.01641) show 68 confirmed IAL failures in 47 production repos that had recursion limits set; framework-induced feedback paths survive the limit when conditional edges re-enter nodes. Treating LangGraph's recursion_limit as complete loop prevention without reviewing agentic loop dependence graphs is flagged as a potential architectural trap.

3. **Model upgrade as reliability fix** — Rabanser et al. (ICML 2026, 2602.16666) explicitly conclude that recent capability gains yield only small reliability improvements. If vQA's primary intervention plan is model escalation (e.g., Haiku → Sonnet → Opus per-step), the evidence suggests this will not materially reduce circuit-breaker firing frequency. Flagged as a potential effort-misdirection trap.

4. **Benchmark success rate inflation** — Xue et al. (COLM 2025, 2504.01382) document that near-90% reported web-agent benchmark rates collapse to 40–55% on realistic tasks because shortcut-exploiting agents game evaluation. Any external "SOTA" success rate claim for web agents should be treated with skepticism unless validated on adversarial / shortcut-free task sets.

### Open Questions

1. **AC quality score floor for execution success**: No paper found that directly maps an AC quality score (e.g., ambiguity level, atomicity score) to autonomous execution success rate with a measured threshold. Yang et al. (2604.21505) show degradation exists but do not report a "below X ambiguity score, success rate drops to near-zero" threshold. This is the DRP-001 open question and remains empirically unresolved in the literature.

2. **SPA-specific navigation budget norms**: No paper found addressing Angular/PrimeNG or equivalent SPA-specific action-budget norms. The production data from Pan et al. (ICML 2026) gives ≤10 steps for 68% of systems, but does not break this down by UI framework or interaction type. SPA stateful navigation may require larger budgets — this is unknown.

3. **Self-correction pattern success rates**: The replan-on-failure loop is described architecturally in multiple sources but no paper reports empirical success rates for self-correction (i.e., what fraction of stuck agents recover via replanning vs. require human escalation). This is a key unknown for deciding whether vQA's circuit-breaker threshold is too tight or too loose.

4. **DOM-programmatic vs. visual-only false-positive differential**: No paper directly compares DOM-programmatic verification to visual-only LLM-judge verification on the same test suite with measured false-positive rates. The evidence that LLM judges have high false-success rates is strong (grade A/B), but the comparative advantage of DOM-first tiering is asserted rather than empirically measured in the literature found.

5. **Environment-specific credential fragility mitigations**: Credential rotation and pre-flight health check patterns appear in security/penetration testing literature (2604.03070) but not in the context of test automation agent environments (role-specific, URL-specific failures). No academic evidence found for the best pre-flight validation pattern in this narrow context — this is likely a practitioner-discourse or OSS lane finding.

6. **LLM-judge/human agreement ceiling by task type**: The 85% LLM-judge/human agreement from Xue et al. is a macro average. Whether this agreement degrades sharply for specific step types (navigation vs. form-fill vs. assertion) is unknown and directly relevant to vQA's verifier design.
